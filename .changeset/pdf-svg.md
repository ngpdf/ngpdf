Add `pdf-svg`, which draws an SVG as vector shapes wherever a `pdf-image` can go. Its `src` takes SVG markup, a `data:image/svg+xml` URL, an http(s) URL (read like an image URL, with `maxImageBytes` and the server's `fetchImage` rule) or a `Blob`. It is sized like an image from the SVG's `width`, `height` and `viewBox`, honours `preserveAspectRatio`, and is clipped to its box. It draws `svg`, `g`, `path` (the whole `d` grammar, arcs included), `rect`, `circle`, `ellipse`, `line`, `polyline`, `polygon`, `use` within the same file, and linear and radial gradients, with fills, strokes, dashes, opacity, `visibility`, the six transform functions and `currentColor`, which is the element's own `color`. Text, images, clip paths, masks, filters, patterns, markers, `symbol` and `<style>` sheets are left out with one development-mode warning each. The parser is ngpdf's own, so it works the same on the server: it reads no DTD or external entities, runs no scripts, fetches no external references, and throws `ngpdf: pdf-svg …` past 1 MB of source, 50,000 elements, 64 levels of nesting, 16 levels of `use` or 1,000,000 path segments and gradient stops drawn. Malformed markup throws `ngpdf: invalid SVG: <reason> at <offset>`.
