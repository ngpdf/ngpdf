Write PDFs as Angular components.

A normal component, a few pdf-* elements, and the browser writes a real PDF: text you can select, tables whose header comes back on every page, page numbers at the bottom. No server and no screenshots.

Read the guide or edit one right here ↓

Angular 22+ · zoneless or zone.js · MIT

Why it exists

At work I looked after three reports that went out as PDFs: a capture of a screen with a title and a timestamp, a list of contacts, and a table of licences. They were built with jsPDF. One was a screenshot of the page, so its text could not be selected; the other two were tables placed by hand, and every new column meant working out coordinates again.

The data was already in Angular components, with pipes, signals and services around it. ngpdf lets that component be the document, and leaves layout, page breaks and the PDF itself to a small engine that loads only when you print.

Gabriel Silva, maintainer

A document is a component

Lay it out with the pdf-* elements and CSS you already know: flexbox, padding, borders, colors. Inputs carry the data, pipes format it, @for repeats it.

Then render it from anywhere you can inject a service.

Edit it, and it prints again

This invoice is an Angular component on this page. Change it and ngpdf lays it out, paginates it and writes a new PDF in your browser. Push the line items past thirty and watch the header follow onto page two.

Specifications

Layout
Flexbox through Yoga: direction, justify, align, gap, grow and shrink.
Tables
Columns declared once in points, percentages or fr. Header rows repeat on every page; rows are never split.
Pages
A3, A4, A5, Letter, Legal or any size, portrait or landscape, with page numbers.
Text
Real text with line breaking, the 14 standard PDF fonts, alignment, decoration.
Images
PNG and JPEG from a URL, a data URL, a Blob or an ArrayBuffer; contain or cover.
Weight
About 2 KB gzipped on your first load. The engine arrives on the first render.
Output
The same input gives the same bytes. No PDF JavaScript, actions or attachments are ever written.
Not yet
Custom fonts, SVG, links, server rendering, splitting a paragraph across pages. They are on the roadmap.
